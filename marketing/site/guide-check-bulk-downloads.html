Batch Grab workflow note · October 22, 2026

A completed download is not a checked document

Reconcile the folder with the files you intended to collect.

A browser can successfully save the wrong response. For example, a link may return a sign-in page instead of the report you expected. A completed transfer alone does not verify its contents.

Use a short reconciliation checklist

  1. Write down the expected documents independently of the downloader’s result count.
  2. Review failed or interrupted transfers separately from completed ones.
  3. Open a sample of saved files. Check the title, format and relevant pages.
  4. Investigate a suspiciously small file or unexpected extension. Do not assume changing its extension repairs its contents.
  5. Retry temporary failures after checking the source. Do not bypass a login, CAPTCHA, subscription or access restriction.

Keep unresolved items visible

A useful handoff says what is missing. Record failed URLs and error details rather than silently removing them from the list.

Batch Grab records Chrome’s completed and interrupted outcomes and provides manual retries. Its source-and-outcome CSV includes actual saved paths and failures. Runtime “completed” means Chrome saved the response; it does not mean Batch Grab verified the PDF or its contents. That distinction matters even when the download process worked correctly.
