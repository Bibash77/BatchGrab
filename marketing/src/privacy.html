Extension data and purpose

When you invoke Batch Grab on a webpage, it reads exposed URLs, link text, nearby labels, headings and image context to identify resources and suggest names. It stores page drafts, selected URLs, filenames, requested and actual saved paths, Chrome download identifiers, status, errors and timestamps in local Chrome extension storage.

Where data goes

The extension has no Batch Grab server, analytics, advertising SDK, cloud naming API or account system. Image previews and downloads make requests to source hosts; source sites and Chrome apply their ordinary session and network behavior. Batch Grab does not bypass access controls.

If you export a source-and-outcome CSV, that file is saved on your device. It can contain private URLs, tokens or local paths. Review it before choosing to share it.

Retention and control

Local history and drafts remain until cleared or the extension is removed. Clear finished history removes finished job records. Removing the extension clears its extension storage but does not delete downloaded files, exported CSV files or Chrome’s own download records.

This website and voluntary contact

No analytics, advertising, cookies or remote form service is enabled here. Request text is prepared in browser memory, with optional copy or local text download. No form submission is sent by the page. If you choose Open email draft and then send through your mail app, your email provider and the publisher receive the message you send. Contact retention and final hosting-log details must be agreed by the owner before publication. A hosting provider may receive ordinary IP/request logs when the site goes live.

Payments
