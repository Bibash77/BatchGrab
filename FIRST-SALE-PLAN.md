# One genuine $5 purchase: the exact next experiment

The delivered product is **Smart Naming and Organized Downloads**, not a spreadsheet downloader. There are no customers or purchases yet. Current beta access is free; do not imply that $5 has been validated. Do not build billing or another feature before testing this workflow.

## Offer and customer

Target one self-purchasing research assistant, bid/document preparer, or independent consultant with an upcoming document-collection task. Qualify by a real task, not a job title: at least 20 accessible documents, unhelpful URL names or generic download labels, a need for a neatly named local folder, and authority to install tools and spend $5. Exclude people whose problem is extracting tables, crawling a portal, changing hosted product media or bypassing access controls.

Offer: **“Collect the documents on this page into useful names and folders, review them before saving, and see what failed. $5 once for the delivered beta version if it works on your real task.”** Specify perpetual use of the purchased beta version; no promise of lifetime site support, future features or a subscription. There is no need to build DRM or an activation service to test one voluntary software purchase. Do not describe the currently free evaluation build as a paid exclusive.

## Days 1–3: reach three opted-in testers

Start with one warm introduction to someone doing the qualifying work, if available. Ask the introducer to forward this invitation rather than handing over a client's contact details:

> I built a Chrome beta that turns webpage document labels into editable filenames and section folders. May I show you a 30-file demo and try it on a current document batch you are allowed to download? The evaluation is free. If it works for your ongoing use, the price I want to test is $5 once. Existing free downloaders may already solve your task; we will compare them too.

If there is no warm route, request permission to share this specific beta offer in one relevant research/VA community. Record moderator permission and recruit only volunteers. Do not cold-message authors of old support posts. The previously checked r/smallbusiness promotion thread permits product offers but prohibits market-research/focus-group solicitation; it is not permission to recruit interviews there. A reviewed store release can later be promoted through a permitted concrete product offer.

Access gate: if three suitable people cannot be reached within seven days and three founder hours, stop acquisition work and obtain one genuine introduction before writing more code. No response is a channel result, not a feature rejection.

## Days 3–7: observe a real task and make the paid ask

1. Show the 30-PDF demo, including closing and reopening the popup. Explain that nearby-label detection is heuristic and the same-page comparison is an authored fixture, not proof of general time savings.
2. Have the tester choose a current, permissioned, non-sensitive page and a 10-document subset. Let them use their normal free downloader first; configure its link-text mask where available. Do not deliberately leave it in a worse default state.
3. Time setup, naming review/corrections, download completion and subsequent folder cleanup separately. Count wrong/missing names and files. Test the same files with Batch Grab; alternate tool order between testers to reduce learning effects. Do not upload private source links to external tools without consent.
4. Ask them to edit at least one name, start the batch, close the popup and inspect outputs. Verify the actual content of several files. Save their measured observations and verbatim objections with permission.
5. Ask plainly: **“For your next batch, would you choose this version for $5 once over your current free option?”** If yes, name the next task and offer the delivered-version license. Praise, an email address or a conditional promise is not a sale.

A checkout is intentionally not part of this implementation. Once a real buyer asks to proceed, use an existing legitimate invoice/payment-link service only after the owner authorizes external setup and seller access, fees and delivery are confirmed. Deliver the versioned ZIP/store build plus a receipt specifying version, scope and refund terms. Do not mark an unpaid invoice as revenue. If a trusted delivery/payment route is unavailable, keep this as an unproven purchase commitment, not a successful commercial experiment.

## Success, failure and next decision

- **Success:** one target customer, not the founder or a supportively buying friend, pays $5 for using the delivered software, receives it, completes a real task and keeps the purchase. Record gross payment, fee, refund status, delivery and support minutes. This meets the $1 revenue threshold for that month; it is not recurring monthly revenue.
- **Failure:** after three qualified real-task trials, no buyer pays, or the free tool is adequate and preferred. Do not add another paid feature. Classify whether naming errors, installation friction, trust, lack of recurring tasks, price or free-tool sufficiency caused rejection.
- **Inconclusive:** no qualified trials or a payment/delivery-access blocker. Fix that bottleneck; do not describe demand as disproved or proven.

Cap this experiment at seven days and five founder hours excluding already completed development. Offer a refund if the delivered beta cannot perform the agreed supported task. This is an experimental policy, not a substitute for applicable customer rights.

After the first sale, ask permission for a factual testimonial/referral, publish one measured before/after example, and repeat the same route for a sale in each of the next two months. Request a Web Store review only from an actual user and never condition payment, refunds or free access on a positive review. Defer a $9–$19 bundle until repeat usage and additional requests justify it.
